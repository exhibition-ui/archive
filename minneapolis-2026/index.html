The People’s Museum for Prince

Minneapolis 2026

In June 2026, the People’s Museum presented a commemorative edition marking ten years since the passing of Prince Rogers Nelson. Here you can meet the people and their stories in the Living Archive, visit the three exhibitions and look back on the events.

Curator’s welcome

Prince was famously disciplined and famously prolific. He dedicated his life wholly to his creative spirit, and his example continues to inspire artists and creative people everywhere. His music, his style, his quiet, strong commitment to social justice, his deep spirituality, his bold explorations of sexuality are some of the ways his influence is felt by people all around the world. The joy his music and performances inspired, and the lasting grief his death triggered, have changed many lives. The bonds within the purple community, forged through shared love, grow stronger year by year.

This museum exists as a gathering point and a mirror, to reflect back the impact of a single artist on countless lives — a testament to how deeply art matters.

The museum is presented in 2026 in the heart of the neighborhood where Prince grew up. You can see Bernadette Anderson's house from the gallery window — the house where Prince lived as a teenager, formed his first band with friends and neighbors, and got seriously started with his music. Bernadette kept him in school. She gave him structure when his own family couldn't. Maybe she saw who he was becoming. Or maybe it wasn't so much about Prince being special, but about Bernadette herself — looking out for a sensitive, talented, troubled teen.

In the last years of his life, Prince began to turn his gaze back to his roots. He stood outside his old houses, taking photographs. He reached out to old friends from early days. He started writing his memoir, The Beautiful Ones. It seemed the arc of his life was bending towards his way back home.

This exhibition takes its title from that return. At this ten year anniversary of life on Earth without Prince, we seek him where we can. We can't find him in his houses, and can't really find him anywhere in Minnesota, though we try. Though he is gone, his creative spirit is strongly present, and dwells in his music, and in our hearts.

No one can fully measure the influence of an artist and how much they mattered. The art and stories gathered here gesture towards a collective testimony, and in some way are gifts offered back to Prince, for all he gave us. I hope your visit brings you home to your own Prince story and the transformative power of art in our lives.

E.C.Balázs, Curator
