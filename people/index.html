People

The People's Museum for Prince is built by people: artists, poets, curators, musicians, historians, worldwide Prince community members and volunteers who believe Prince's legacy belongs to all of us.

With our hearts in Minneapolis, we work together as an international community from all around the world, to honor Prince Rogers Nelson and pay tribute to the way art changes lives.

Founding Curator

At Roberts Gallery, Minneapolis 2026 · Photo: Kirk Thoren

Emma Balázs

Founding Curator

Emma Balázs is an Australian curator and filmmaker who founded The People's Museum for Prince after witnessing the profound community response to Prince's death in 2016. With a PhD in Curatorial and Museum Studies from the University of Melbourne and a Masters from the School of the Art Institute of Chicago, she brings two decades of experience across leading international art institutions in Australia, the US and Singapore.

Her "counter museum" approach puts community voices ahead of institutional narratives, creating collective portraits through personal stories, artwork and objects. The first People's Museum, in 2018, welcomed over 3,000 visitors during Minneapolis's Art-A-Whirl weekend. The 2026 edition, marking ten years since the passing of Prince Rogers Nelson, brought 75 participants from 9 countries to three exhibitions in Minneapolis, and lives on in the Living Archive.

Emma has presented her work at University of Minnesota Prince symposiums and MoPop Seattle, and contributed a chapter to Blackstar Rising and the Purple Reign: The Sonic Afterlives of David Bowie and Prince (Duke University Press, 2026), edited by Daphne Brooks. Originally trained as a filmmaker at the Victorian College of the Arts, she makes films as E.C.Balázs.

In Minneapolis

The people who trusted the vision, opened their doors and welcomed the museum into the community.

Photos: courtesy of Peyton Scott Russell and Hawona Sullivan Janzen; courtesy of E.C.Balázs (the Capri); Jill Boogren (Tammy Ortegon)

Peyton Scott Russell

Roberts Gallery, North Minneapolis · 2026

Minneapolis graffiti artist and educator Peyton Scott Russell founded Juxtaposition Arts and, after Prince's death, transformed his star at First Avenue in gold leaf. In 2025 he took over Homewood Studios from his mentor George Roberts and renamed it Roberts Gallery. In June 2026 he opened it to the museum, bringing Way Back Home: Prince and North Minneapolis to Prince's own neighborhood.

In the Living Archive → · Roberts Gallery →

Hawona Sullivan Janzen

Artist, writer and curator · Twin Cities

A deeply established Twin Cities artist, writer and curator of Prince exhibitions including Where Purple Reigns: Minnesota Artists on Prince. Hawona was part of the first museum in 2018 and returned in 2026, reading her poem at the Capri. Her trust in the museum's vision, and her introduction to Peyton Scott Russell, brought the museum to Roberts Gallery.

Historian · Houses of Prince

A historian, archaeologist and preservationist who researches, writes and leads tours about places associated with Prince and the Minneapolis Sound. Kristen and Emma have worked together since 2018 on Houses of Prince, the museum's ongoing curatorial research project.
