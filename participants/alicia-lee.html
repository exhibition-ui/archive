Innocence and Prince
probably nine was too young to be listening to Controversy and coordinating dance routines to his upbeat Private Joy we rewound the cassette so many times how the delicate ribbon with his magic could withstand the smashing of boombox buttons is testament to his loving power when I was twenty and probably too young some of my wolf pack persuaded me to Chanhassen where his Paisley Park studio held court the lobby disguised as an odd prom with disco dots alighting office furniture and potted plants we were patted down at the door drugs and guns were not allowed in his majesty's kingdom I became part of the wall and watched Henry the Fifth in purple and his announcing boots take laps with his alert bodyguards surveying his subjects they danced to Housequake and Raspberry Beret each disciple trying to prove loyalty through their Elvis hips and eager puppy smiles hoping for just an approving nod from the creator from the god who walked among us and left us anointed in his sexy grace

Alicia Lee is a Wisconsin-based poet and certified sommelier. Her work is featured or forthcoming in Slipstream, Trashlight, and One Art. She is a contributor to the Voices of Neighbors anthology and first place recipient in the Lakefly Writing Contest. She grew up in Minnesota, listening to Prince and dancing at First Avenue. Alicia performed a reading of her poem for the community as part of the Forever in My Life film and art night at the Capri on June 10, 2026.
